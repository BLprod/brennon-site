The short version

I didn't start running mountains to find answers. If anything, I started with more questions than I knew what to do with — and somewhere on a climb that wouldn't end, I noticed the questions got quieter. Not answered. Just quieter.

Ultra marathons in the mountains are my thing now — not because I'm chasing a finish line, but because the mountain has a way of stripping things back to what's actually true: your breath, your legs, your thoughts, in that order. I'm still figuring out who I am out here. Still asking why. I've just stopped expecting the trail to answer quickly.

Somewhere along the way, stoic philosophy started making a lot more sense than it used to. Not as a performance mindset — more as permission. Permission to suffer well, to let go of what I can't control, and to keep showing up without needing it to look impressive.

Off the trail, I've spent twenty-plus years building software — a different kind of long game. I don't know yet if there's a real throughline between that and any of this. Maybe there is. I'm still finding it, same as everything else here.

This is a record of that, kept honestly. No finish-line hero shots pretending it was easy. Just the climbing.

The dichotomy of control

Epictetus's oldest exercise, still the hardest one. Some things are up to me. Most things aren't. Here's mine, still being sorted — try it with something of yours.

In my control

  • The next step
  • How I respond to the cramp
  • Whether I show up tomorrow
  • The effort, not the outcome

Not in my control

  • The weather on race day
  • The mountain's terrain
  • Other runners' pace
  • Whether the watch battery lasts
